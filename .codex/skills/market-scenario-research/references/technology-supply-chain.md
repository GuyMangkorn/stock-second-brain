# Technology And Supply-Chain Scenarios

Use this reference for AI, semiconductors, power, data centers, industrial
capacity, shortages, and bottleneck migration.

## Bottleneck Test

A durable bottleneck usually has several of these properties:

- demand grows faster than usable capacity
- capacity has a long permitting, qualification, construction, or ramp time
- few substitutes meet the required performance or location
- the constrained input is a small cost but blocks a much larger system
- backlog, lead time, utilization, or price remains elevated after announced
  capacity additions

Distinguish a physical shortage from allocation, qualification, logistics,
regulation, location, or economics.

## Causal Map

Trace only the relevant layers:

1. End demand and workload growth
2. Compute, memory, packaging, networking, or storage
3. Rack integration, power delivery, cooling, and buildings
4. Generation, grid connection, transformers, permitting, and fuel
5. Software utilization, inference cost, customer ROI, and willingness to pay

The next bottleneck is normally the slowest dependency after the current
constraint receives capacity. Check whether demand, architecture, or efficiency
improvements can move or eliminate it.

## Preferred Evidence

Prioritize official company capacity plans and filings, regulator or grid data,
government energy and trade statistics, technical architecture documents, and
industry bodies. Use news to date events or fill context gaps.

## Signposts

Choose three to five that can change the thesis:

- lead times, utilization, backlog, or allocation policies
- announced capacity versus qualified usable output
- capex, construction, permitting, or interconnection timing
- price and gross-margin response across the value chain
- architecture, efficiency, substitution, or demand changes
- customer utilization, inference economics, and realized ROI

## Scenario Discipline

- `Base`: current bottleneck eases on announced timelines; the slowest adjacent
  dependency becomes binding.
- `Bull / Upside`: demand or monetization exceeds the capacity response.
- `Bear / Downside`: supply arrives faster, efficiency improves, or end demand
  fails to justify deployed capacity.

Name the first observation that would falsify the base case.
