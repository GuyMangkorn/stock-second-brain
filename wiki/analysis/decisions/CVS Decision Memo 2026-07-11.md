## Decision-Changing Evidence

Q1 revenue rose 6.2% to USD 100.426bn, operating income rose 38.7% to USD 4.680bn, and adjusted EPS rose to USD 2.57. The strongest proof was Health Care Benefits: adjusted operating income rose 52.6% to USD 3.041bn and MBR improved 270bp to 84.6%. Management raised FY2026 GAAP EPS to USD 6.24-6.44, adjusted EPS to USD 7.30-7.50, revenue to at least USD 405bn, and OCF to at least USD 9.5bn.

Quality is not one-directional. Favorable prior-year health-cost development was USD 1.1bn, so MBR improvement is not equivalent to purely current-period underwriting improvement. Medical membership fell to 26.0m, partly from exiting individual exchange. Management also retained caution on elevated cost trends. Q2 must show whether recovery survives a more demanding utilization environment.

The other engines are mixed. Health Services revenue grew 11.0%, but adjusted operating income fell 7.1% as client price improvements compressed economics. Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness prescriptions grew 3.6%, yet adjusted operating income fell 8.8% due to reimbursement pressure, investment, seasonality, and weather. This is why top-line growth alone is not the thesis.

Cash conversion is adequate but volatile: FY2025 FCF was USD 7.807bn versus USD 6.326bn in FY2024 and USD 10.395bn in FY2023; Q1 2026 FCF fell 10.8% YoY to USD 3.400bn. Debt of USD 63.111bn against cash USD 9.542bn makes sustained FCF and debt reduction decision-critical.

## Valuation Read

P11 passed with high sensitivity. The consolidated DCF uses USD 7.0bn base starting FCF, 8% five-year growth, 8.5% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth, net debt USD 53.569bn, and 1.279bn diluted shares. Base fair value is USD 76.47; bear USD 22.60; bull USD 142.85. Terminal value is 77.2% of base EV. Because CVS includes an insurer, this is a scenario read; a later SOTP should allocate insurance capital and segment economics.

The multiple cross-check is less expensive than the DCF: 13.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint and 6.0% trailing FCF yield. The divergence is explained mainly by leverage and the insurance balance-sheet treatment. It argues against calling CVS obviously overvalued, but it does not create a base-case margin of safety at USD 102.83.

## Key Assumption / Falsifier

The key assumption is that Aetna's improvement is durable after stripping reserve development and that pressure in Caremark/retail stops worsening. Upgrade evidence: Q2/Q3 MBR stays near target, current-period cost trend is controlled, OCF/capex supports at least FY2025-like FCF, and debt falls. Falsifiers: renewed MBR deterioration, another guidance cut, Health Services/retail profit erosion despite revenue growth, or capital allocation that prevents deleveraging.
