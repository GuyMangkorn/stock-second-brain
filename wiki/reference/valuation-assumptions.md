# Valuation Assumptions

Use this reference for DCF and scenario valuation work. These are starting
points, not automatic conclusions.

## WACC By Sector

| Sector | Typical WACC Range | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Communication Services | 8%-10% | Mix of stable telecom and growth media. |
| Consumer Discretionary | 8%-10% | Cyclical exposure. |
| Consumer Staples | 7%-8% | Defensive, stable demand. |
| Energy | 9%-11% | Commodity price exposure. |
| Financials | 8%-10% | Leverage is part of the business model; simple DCF may be less appropriate. |
| Health Care | 8%-10% | Regulatory and pipeline risk. |
| Industrials | 8%-9% | Moderate cyclicality. |
| Information Technology | 8%-12% | Assess growth stage and reinvestment intensity. |
| Materials | 8%-10% | Cyclical and commodity exposure. |
| Real Estate | 7%-9% | Interest-rate sensitivity. |
| Utilities | 6%-7% | Regulated, stable cash flows. |

## Adjustment Factors

Add to base WACC:

| Factor | Adjustment |
|---|---:|
| High leverage | +1%-2% |
| Small cap or limited liquidity | +1%-2% |
| Emerging market exposure | +1%-3% |
| Customer concentration | +0.5%-1% |
| Regulatory uncertainty | +0.5%-1.5% |
| Cyclical or volatile FCF | +0.5%-2% |

Subtract from base WACC:

| Factor | Adjustment |
|---|---:|
| Market leader with durable moat | -0.5%-1% |
| Recurring revenue model | -0.5%-1% |
| Investment-grade credit profile | -0.5% |
| Net cash balance sheet | -0.25%-0.75% |

## Terminal Growth

| Business Type | Terminal Growth Range |
|---|---:|
| Mature developed-market compounder | 2.0%-3.0% |
| GDP-like defensive company | 1.5%-2.5% |
| Cyclical business | 1.0%-2.5% |
| High-growth company fading to maturity | 2.0%-3.0% after explicit fade |

## Sanity Checks

- Terminal value should usually be 50%-85% of enterprise value for mature
  companies.
- If terminal value is above 90% of enterprise value, the DCF is highly
  assumption-sensitive.
- Fair value should be compared with rough valuation cross-checks such as FCF
  yield, EV/FCF, P/E, EV/revenue, or peer multiples when source data exists.
- If ROIC is below WACC, be cautious about assuming long-term value creation.
- For banks, insurers, REITs, and highly levered financial businesses, use a
  sector-specific model instead of a simple FCF DCF unless the user explicitly
  requests a rough framework.
